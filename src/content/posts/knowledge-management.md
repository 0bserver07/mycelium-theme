# Personal Knowledge Management

Personal Knowledge Management (PKM) is the practice of organizing, connecting, and cultivating your knowledge.

## Why PKM Matters

In the age of information overload, we consume more than we can remember. PKM helps us:

1. **Capture** ideas before they fade
2. **Connect** disparate concepts
3. **Create** new insights from old notes
4. **Communicate** what we've learned

> [!quote]
> "The faintest ink is more powerful than the strongest memory." — Chinese Proverb

## The Digital Garden Approach

Traditional note-taking is linear. Digital gardens are **networked**.

Instead of files in folders, think of ideas as nodes in a graph. Each note can link to any other, creating emergent structures.

See: [[growth-philosophy|The Philosophy of Growth]]

## Key Principles

### 1. Capture Quickly
Don't overthink the initial capture. Use seedling notes liberally.

### 2. Connect Generously
Link notes even if the connection seems tenuous. Patterns emerge over time.

### 3. Revisit Regularly
Notes need tending. Set aside time to:
- Review recent additions
- Update outdated information
- Strengthen connections

### 4. Share Openly
Publishing your garden creates accountability and invites collaboration.

> [!tip] Start Small
> You don't need a complex system. Start with one folder of markdown files.
